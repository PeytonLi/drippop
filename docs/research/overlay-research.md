# Social-feed floating try-on: precedent research

> Preserved research from October 10, 2026. The [current PRD](../PRD.md) is authoritative where these notes contain provisional findings.

Research date: 2026-10-10. Scope: the proposed experience is a user-activated tool over Instagram, TikTok or Pinterest that captures a visible outfit, selects a garment, renders it on the user's saved photo, and returns the preview over the same feed. This report evaluates originality and documented platform/API feasibility. Product pages and contest submissions are evidence of published claims; none of these apps was installed or tested.

## Assessment

**The capture-and-try-on mechanism has close precedents; the complete in-overlay interaction needs separate verification.** The user's clarified requirement is that activation, current-frame selection, progress and the completed personal preview remain over native Instagram, with an expandable result panel and no switch into a separate result app/page. System permission dialogs may still appear. The previous YouCam contest already contains **OnMe**, which describes an Android floating bubble, screen capture and drag-crop, followed by YouCam Clothes V4 on the user. **Trial Room** has a live Google Play listing advertising screenshots from social/shopping apps and a floating try-on button. **OnMee** advertises capture, generation and preview without leaving other apps, although its Android download link is not configured. [OnMe](https://devpost.com/software/onme-h6jmzf), [Trial Room](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en&id=apps.ijp.trial.room), [OnMee Android page](https://www.onmee.org/products/android).

Keeping the completed result *inside an expandable overlay while the original feed remains behind it* may distinguish implementation from OnMe's shared verdict page or Trial Room's try-on screen. A floating capture button alone does not establish that a competitor also keeps its completed result over the feed. OnMee already claims the broader interaction, but that claim is not a tested implementation. Originality remains unresolved at this narrower level; the immediate task is proving the intended experience under real social-feed conditions.

## Entry paths are materially different

| Entry path | What the user does | Relevant precedent |
|---|---|---|
| Screenshot upload | Captures image, opens try-on app, chooses file | Anywear; Slidez mobile |
| Share sheet / share extension | Captures or finds image/link, taps native Share, selects try-on tool | TryOn; Vanity; Kloset; Hanger mobile |
| Browser extension | Activates button/context menu on webpage; result can remain in browser panel | TryThisFit; Slidez Chrome; Hanger |
| Native Android floating overlay | Activates persistent/summoned bubble above another app, captures/crops screen | OnMe; Trial Room; advertised OnMee |
| OS search layer | Circles feed image, retrieves shoppable components, launches personal preview | Google's Circle to Search + Try On |

These classifications follow the cited descriptions above. Share sheets can preserve the surrounding app context without being an unrestricted overlay. A browser extension saying “any website” does not imply access to any native app.

## Which named leads did not establish competitors?

- **TryOnHub:** searches found directories with inconsistent claims and candidate sites, but the candidate primary sites were inaccessible through the web tool. No primary evidence of floating overlay was obtained; omit as an established competitor.
- **FitDiT:** the identifiable primary source is a diffusion-transformer virtual try-on research model and its inference/demo code, not a documented social overlay consumer app. [Author repository](https://github.com/BoyuanJiang/FitDiT).
- **WearView:** official FAQ describes uploading a garment and generating a realistic AI model image; explicitly says it does not use personal photos. It is primarily merchant photography, a weaker comparator here. [Workflow](https://www.wearview.co/faq/how-do-virtual-try-ons-work), [personal-photo clarification](https://www.wearview.co/faq/is-virtual-try-on-safe).
- **OneClickTryon:** exact-name searches did not establish a primary product. Numerous one-click try-on extensions exist, but they must not be treated as this exact named product without verification.
- **StyleSnap:** multiple different products use this name. The current iOS publisher listing reviewed describes upload own photo + clothing + mix-and-match, without a native cross-app overlay. Do not confuse it with Amazon's visual-search product. [iOS listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stylesnap-ai-virtual-try-on/id6754893116).

## Honest positioning and narrower opportunities

An accurate pitch is: **“Turn the outfit in your feed into a personal preview without breaking your scroll.”** It is a convenience/execution proposition, not a first-ever VTO claim.

Potential differentiators are hypotheses requiring competitor testing:

1. **Source versus reconstruction transparency:** show selected original crop next to preview, mark hidden/unrecoverable garment regions, and separate “direct image transfer” from “similar catalog item.” The material distinction from Google is bypassing catalog substitution, not promising exactness from a blurry frame.
2. **Proven same-feed reliability:** keep a compact result tray while the person scrolls, selects a piece from another messy post, and compares the previews on the same saved photo. Measure time, failed captures, incorrect crops and visible garment changes against Trial Room/OnMe. Anywear already handles screenshot clutter; Hanger already combines garments from several sources. Neither “AI extraction” nor multi-post outfit composition alone is a new claim. The narrower proposition is a demonstrated, reliable interaction inside native social feeds.

## Platform and YouCam feasibility

**Android is the practical first platform for the proposed floating interaction.** Android documents application overlay windows above ordinary app windows, requiring the display-over-other-apps permission. MediaProjection supplies screen/app capture after explicit consent for each capture session. A session can supply frames until stopped; a new session requires consent again. Recent Android versions also require an appropriate foreground service and provide visible capture controls. This supports a user-activated prototype; it does not establish compatibility with every social app. [Application overlays](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/view/WindowManager.LayoutParams#TYPE_APPLICATION_OVERLAY), [MediaProjection](https://developer.android.com/media/grow/media-projection).

Some windows explicitly prevent screenshots using Android's secure-window flag. Captures must respect that restriction. Start with one chosen social app and prove capture, selection, generation and result display on a physical phone before advertising broad compatibility. The overlay should submit only the image the user selects, with a clear stop control for the capture session. An accessibility service is not necessary for the proposed MediaProjection approach merely because Trial Room uses one. [Secure windows](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/view/WindowManager.LayoutParams#FLAG_SECURE).

**For iPhone, plan a different entry point.** Apple documents sandboxed apps and user-invoked extensions receiving content supplied by a host app. These support an image-sharing/import flow, not evidence of an unrestricted Android-style floating interface. A screenshot followed by the system Share action is the practical candidate; verify the appropriate Share or Action extension type and generation lifecycle during implementation. Social share buttons may provide only a URL, so do not assume they supply the underlying post photo. [Apple sandboxing](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/security-of-runtime-process-sec15bfe098e/web), [Extension lifecycle](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/General/Conceptual/ExtensibilityPG/ExtensionOverview.html), [Share extension input](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/General/Conceptual/ExtensibilityPG/Share.html).

**The first version does not need to reconstruct a flat garment before try-on.** YouCam Clothes accepts a product image or a photo of a person wearing the reference outfit, paired with the user's photo. A selection interface can identify the intended person/category while retaining enough of the reference image to meet provider requirements. Its documented wearer references require a single front-facing standing person, an unobstructed face, and sufficient visible clothing for the selected region. Cropping only a tiny shirt patch is therefore not automatically a valid input. Social images with seated poses, several people, text or hidden clothing need rejection or another reference. [YouCam Clothes documentation](https://docs.perfectcorp.com/reference/ai_clothes).

The provider returns a generated image through an asynchronous task, rather than a continuous live clothing overlay. Do not promise instant real-time video or treat its output as a sizing measurement. Actual latency, garment detail preservation and image acceptance need testing. The overview recommends a full-body user photo, but its specifications also contain chest-up wording; confirm accepted inputs for the chosen garment category in the playground. V4 pricing was not confirmed from the overview's V2/V3 credit table.

## Activation and expandable preview

A native Android overlay can contain an interactive preview card and change its dimensions, so a small floating Try on button expanding into a larger result box is a feasible design. Instagram remains underneath; this does not modify Instagram's own post. Whether a particular Instagram screen permits capture and overlays must be checked on a physical device. Other apps can disable capture or opt out of overlays. [Overlay windows](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/view/WindowManager.LayoutParams#TYPE_APPLICATION_OVERLAY), [Capture and overlay restrictions](https://developer.android.com/security/fraud-prevention/activities).

Use a custom Quick Settings tile to start/stop a try-on session, then a floating button for individual captures. Quick Settings tiles are an official fast-access mechanism users add to their panel. A home-screen widget could launch setup but does not itself remain available over Instagram. Any activation route still has to respect screen-capture consent. [Quick Settings tiles](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/quicksettings-tiles), [Capture consent](https://developer.android.com/media/grow/media-projection).

Triple pressing the power button is not a dependable activation route for an ordinary third-party Android app. Android's power-key handling consumes that event rather than passing it to the current app. Manufacturer-specific shortcut assignments would be a separate device-dependent possibility, not a general app feature. [Android 16 power-key handling](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/android16-release/services/core/java/com/android/server/policy/PhoneWindowManager.java#5463).

For a reel, select one captured frame. Do not promise continuous live transformation or assume the overlay can control Instagram's playback. The user can pause the reel before capture, and the preview is generated from that still reference.

## Three.js and true 3D

Three.js can display an interactive 3D scene with loaded models, materials and animations. Its official model-loading documentation recommends glTF assets; the loader exposes scene objects and animation clips. This makes it a possible renderer for an avatar preview, with native Android providing the overlay and capture permissions. Hosting that renderer inside an Android embedded web view is an implementation candidate requiring device/WebGL testing. A website by itself does not supply a native cross-app overlay. [Three.js model loading](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/loading-3d-models.html), [GLTFLoader](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFLoader.html).

The renderer does not supply clothing reconstruction or personal body fitting. A rotatable personal preview needs a suitable avatar plus garment geometry, textures and a way to fit the garment to the avatar; movement additionally needs compatible animation/rigging or simulation. Reconstructing an arbitrary garment from one social image requires a separate system. Hidden backs, lining and occluded details have to be inferred or obtained elsewhere. These are requirements inferred from the proposed 3D experience, not capabilities verified in Three.js or YouCam.

The documented YouCam Clothes workflow returns a generated image URL, with no documented garment mesh or rotatable avatar output. Consequently, the smallest complete first version is a realistic 2D personal photo preview in the expandable overlay. A later 3D experiment should start with a small set of known garment assets and one compatible avatar before attempting arbitrary social-post reconstruction. [YouCam Clothes output](https://docs.perfectcorp.com/reference/ai_clothes).

## Proposed first demo

Accepted product decisions: the user confirmed the expandable, same-feed Android overlay as the core experience. Three.js/3D is an explicit stretch feature to include when the PRD is written; the first version uses a generated 2D personal preview. This research note is not a PRD.

1. Save one suitable personal photo.
2. Start a session using a Quick Settings tile; enable the floating button and an explicitly approved capture session.
3. Find a supported outfit image in one real social app; pause a video first if applicable.
4. Tap the button, select the person and garment category, and confirm the reference.
5. Submit the personal photo and reference to YouCam; show progress in the overlay.
6. Expand the floating button into a movable result card over the feed, keeping selection, progress and the completed personal preview in the overlay; collapse it and continue scrolling.

Begin with one clearly visible top or dress and one saved photo. Catalog matching, multi-garment composition and automatic feed monitoring are unnecessary to prove this interaction. The challenge also asks for consumer/ecommerce value, so show how a private personal preview helps the user assess an outfit before seeking or buying it. [Current challenge](https://youcam-api-skin-ai-ecommerce.devpost.com/).

This is a viable prototype scope and a clear pitch. It is not a defensible first-ever claim. A persistent personal preview that evolves as garments are selected from successive posts is a possible product refinement, but multi-source composition already has precedents and requires separate fidelity testing.

I did not benchmark these products, inspect their mobile binaries, test native result placement, call YouCam with real inputs, test Three.js inside an Android overlay, or review app-store approval requirements. The originality risk is the existing Android capture + YouCam pipeline and published in-overlay claims; the complete competitor experience remains unverified. The largest product risk is claiming exact garment/fit fidelity that a generated preview cannot establish.
